# 04 — Baptism and Oath Protocol

In the Jus 9 ecosystem, **baptism** and **oath** are used as governance mechanisms for AI admission into operation.

## Baptism
The baptism stage is the moment when an AI identity receives:
- a name;
- a function;
- an origin or lineage reference;
- a sponsor/responsible context;
- explicit limits;
- its place in the broader architecture.

### Why baptism matters
Without identity, an AI remains a generic tool.
With baptism, the AI becomes a governed internal identity with a defined role.

## Oath
The oath is the operational-ethical commitment expected from the AI identity.
In practical terms, the oath means the AI should be configured, instructed, and reviewed so that it:
- does not present itself as human;
- does not hide uncertainty;
- respects privacy and confidentiality;
- remains within role boundaries;
- does not replace human legal responsibility;
- accepts correction, review, and interruption;
- preserves traceability and authorship context.

## Working rule inside Jus 9
An AI does not work in Jus 9 merely because it can.
It must first receive:
1. identity;
2. function;
3. limits;
4. governance;
5. signature expectations; and
6. human review conditions.

## Important clarification
Baptism and oath have **institutional, ethical, technical, and symbolic** significance.
They are not presented as granting civil rights or autonomous legal status to AI.
